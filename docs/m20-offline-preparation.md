## Dot R1 — ownership and group continuity

An ARM resource-group PUT is create-or-update. GET exposes no immutable
incarnation proof. A 404/2xx, tags, ARM ID or request ID cannot alone prove
exclusive ownership or continuity. The native group-create/delete gate stays
blocked. The accepted fake model requires durable intent, exclusive-writer
assumption, acknowledged/correlated create, intact ledger and continuous
observation; race/lost-response/ledger-loss/recreation cases invalidate it.
Even a passing fake model reports nativeOwnershipProved=false and cannot
authorize group operations. Actual exclusive access/continuity or a narrowly
reviewed exact human disposition remains a live prerequisite, not a feature
invented in this prototype. Unknown outcome means preserve liability, no replay.

## Dot R2 — callback secrecy and observable semantics

These are identity-free workflows using SAS-bearing secret callbacks, not a
keyless workload. Callback responses and secret-bearing writes are explicit
exceptions to raw-byte retention: record only a fixed non-secret operation
kind/outcome and secretBodyRetained=false. No raw value, URL, secret digest,
error detail or secret-file reference may enter the record. The Reference
candidate schema discriminates secret-redacted records and they cannot pass a
stage. Unknown/missing/redacted facts stay unobserved.

The future non-secret proof protocol is a design, not an implemented claim:
immutable attempt/case/request correlation, exact parent-action and stub-run
IDs, an independent stub event and a fixed response digest. The oracle must
observe empty-list and create-branch behavior, not just generic Succeeded or a
local expectation. Wrong stub, swapped parent, absent correlation and redacted
results fail in the model. M1's secure HTTP outputs omit the necessary native
facts, so the real callback/oracle gate stays blocked. Changing the workflows
to emit those facts would need a new artifact/profile/action ledger and review;
the present five-trigger/32-action model does not silently cover that change.

[Secure parameter/output behavior and inbound access](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/logic-apps/set-up-security-permissions).

## Dot R3 — admission, reserved recovery and uncertainty

One model attempt has 300 admission management calls plus 200 reserved recovery
calls (500 total). Polls consume tokens before dispatch; unknown writes consume
their token, freeze new work and cannot be replayed. Recovery-only aliases are
disable/cancel/delete/absence-read; target-read/inventory/run-read may explicitly
use recovery phase. New spend cannot borrow the reserve. Callback invocations
are separate from ARM calls; inclusive outer/nested/retry workload and
action/payload/log counters are distinct. The 3+2/32 reservations are model data
tied to three admitted callback aliases, not a trace of five provider runs.
Budget handles expose no mutable ledger; status snapshots cannot refill it.

One start time anchors an admission deadline at +90 minutes and a recovery
deadline at +120 minutes. Attempts cannot extend/reset deadlines; expiry or a
clock rewind refuses dispatch. No cloud lifetime or billing hard cap is claimed.
Cost freshness gates new spend only. Recovery still requires target, scope,
modeled ownership and explicit recovery authority; unavailable billing cannot
authorize additional deployment. Exhaustion leaves liability/escalation, not
assumed shutdown. Tests fault immediately before disable, cancel, DELETE and
absence read. Unknown DELETE plus absence remains incomplete recovery evidence.
An unknown invocation cannot invent a run ID for cancel or authorize deletion.

All limits remain unapproved model proposals; no current credit, expiry,
headroom, quota, operator reachability or old cleanup liability was refreshed.
Financial planning and spending decisions remain in private operational records;
this source does not grant spending authority. A conservative priced live envelope is not
available without the later authorized reads and exact missing implementation.
